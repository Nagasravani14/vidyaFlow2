11.2.1 Defining Trigonometric Ratios
In the above activity, when we observe right angled triangles ABP, ACQ, ADR and
AES,  A  is common,  B,  C,  D and  E are right angles and  P, ,  Q,  R and
 S are also equal (why?).  Hence, we can say that triangles ABP, ACQ, ADR and AES
are similar triangles.  When we observe the ratio of opposite side of angle A and hypotenuse in a
right angled triangle and the ratio of corresponding sides in remaining triangles, is found to be
constant.
The ratios BP CQ DR, ,AP AQ AR   and  ESAS  are named as “sine A A” or simply “sin A A” in those
triangles.  If the value of angle A is ‘’, then the ratio would be  “sin ”.
Hence, we can conclude that the ratio of opposite side of an angle (other than right angle)
and length of the hypotenuse is constant in all similar right angled triangles. This ratio will
be named as “sine” of that angle.
Similarly, when we observe the ratios
AB AC AD, ,AP AQ AR  and  AEAS , it is also found to be
constant.  And these are the ratios of the adjacent sides of the angle A and hypotenuses in right
angled triangles ABP, ACQ, ADR and AES.  So, the ratios
AB AC AD, ,AP AQ AR  and  AEAS
will be named as “cosine A” or simply “cos A” in those triangles.  If the value of the angle A is
“”, then the ratio would be “cos ”
Hence, we can also conclude that the ratio of the adjacent side of an angle (other than
right angle)  and length of the hypotenuse is constant in all similar right triangles.  This
ratio will be named as “cosine” of that angle.
Similarly, the ratio of opposite side and adjacent side of an angle is constant and it can be
named as “tangent” of that angle.
